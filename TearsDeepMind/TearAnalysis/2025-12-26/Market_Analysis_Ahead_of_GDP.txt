GDP numbers yesterday came in far stronger than expected, with real GDP up 4.3% vs expectations of 3.3%, and last quarter’s reading of 3.8%. 
GDP price index also came in far stronger than expected, up 3.7% vs 2.7% expected, and 2.1% last quarter. 
Frankly, a strong GDP  reading can hardly be considered a surprise, when we look at how cyclical stocks have been performing in the market. Clearly, they have ben able to sniff out the economic strength amidst recent rate cuts, with copper at multi year highs, financials at the highs and transportation stocks performing well. These real economy names have been leading market strength of late, but I must say that the extent of the beat did catch me off guard. 
Whilst everyone knows the headline numbers, let’s dig into the inner details a little.  
Looking at which components of GDP drove the beat, we see that it was mostly net exports (as a result of the weak dollar) and personal consumption that drove the beat, whilst private inventories remained a drag. 
Image link incase zoom isn't working (I believe if you uninstall and reinstall the app, it should again):https://postimg.cc/jDCgYyJV
Looking at the consumption element, there has been a lot of concern recently that much of the economy’s consumption has come from AI related spend. 
This was certainly the case in Q1 2025, where AI related investment came in positive, whilst other sectors were actually negative. 
However this quarter was totally different, as we see in the chart below:
https://postimg.cc/3WBnytgg
Looking at the chart below, the annualised rate of change in AI investments slowed back to recent baseline. That’s not a concern for the AI industry, as it is only a return to recent baseline, but it does push back on the suggestion that AI is the only thing propping up the economy. 
https://postimg.cc/HVvzzjv6
In fact, total investment in computers & software contributed only 0.3% to the 4.3% GDP growth in Q3. Clearly, the print was more robust than that. 
Looking below, we saw that all but 2 of the categories were positive, with a massive explosion in healthcare spending.

It is worth noting that a non insignificant portion of last quarter’s GDP upside came from the net exports category as a result of falling inputs. This provides a mechanical boost to GDP and is probably putting a bit of an extra shine on the print. 
A cleaner read of the underlying demand is probably final sales to private domestic  purchases, which still came in at a very robust 3.0% (actually the strongest read in a year), but was meaningfully lower than the 4.3% reported.
There is also price data that is released with the GDP print and we also had some data from the quarterly PCE numbers. 
The GDP deflator, measures the change in prices of final goods and services and it is considered as a key indicator for inflationary pressures, that can give some insight into future monetary policy decisions.
Yesterday, it came in WAY ahead of expectations at 3.7% vs 2.7% expected. 
The PCE numbers that accompanied it came out in line, but accelerated vs Q2 to 2.8% vs 2.1% last quarter, and core came in at 2.9% vs 2.6% last quarter.

Higher GDP and higher prices may see an initial drop, but again, not a bad outcome, since we know the Fed is currently dismissing inflation concerns as temporary. 
This is pretty much what we saw as US500 fell to 6860 in premarket, before stabilising and pushing higher. 
The headline growth was stronger than I anticipated it would be, but the 3% domestic purchases is probably a more appropriate read. The price data came in much stronger than I anticipated. 
Overall, it was a good print for the economy, but wasn’t the best print for the market, as such robust growth, coupled with higher GDP Deflator numbers doesn’t exactly pressure the Fed to cut. With growth so strong, the Fed may now feel they have room to pause at the January meeting, and this is what the market priced in, with odds of a January rate cut falling from 18% to 13%.
